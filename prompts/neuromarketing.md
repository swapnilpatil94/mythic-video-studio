# KATHAAYA Story Psychology — Short + Longform

This is a shared attention framework, not a Shorts-only "retention hack". The same psychological vocabulary is used across formats; the pacing, loop depth, payoff distance and visual rhythm change by format.

## Shared principles

Use curiosity, open loops, information withholding, tension escalation, emotional identification, surprise/reversal, micro-payoffs, pattern interruption and memorable endings. Never manufacture outrage, fake uncertainty, or unsupported mythology. Every open loop must eventually receive a truthful payoff.

## SHORT — 60–90 seconds

Primary objective: earn the next second.

- Start with a concrete unanswered question, contradiction, danger, or surprising consequence.
- Maintain high information density without rushing narration.
- Usually keep 1–3 active open loops.
- Use micro-payoffs roughly every 6–10 seconds.
- Use strong visual or rhythmic interruptions when attention starts to flatten.
- Prefer partial reveals: hands → object → reaction → face → full context.
- Save the strongest artwork transformation and emotional beat for the reveal/climax.
- End with a memorable insight, reversal, question, or share/save-worthy detail.
- Do not turn every beat into a zoom, keyword, or transition; variation is itself an attention tool.

Typical visual rhythm: meaningful change every ~0.5–2 seconds, achieved mainly through crops, shot changes, action, camera and layered motion rather than generating a new image for every change.

## LONGFORM — 8–15+ minutes

Primary objective: create emotional investment in a journey, not continuous sensory stimulation.

Use a hierarchy of questions:

1. **Outer question** — the central mystery the episode promises to answer.
2. **Scene questions** — smaller questions that move the story forward.
3. **Emotional question** — what the viewer begins to feel or judge about the character.
4. **Payoff** — answer a question, then naturally open the next one.

Longform should use nested open loops rather than dozens of rapid loops. Keep roughly 2–4 meaningful active loops and track them explicitly. Payoffs should occur at multiple scales:

- micro payoff: roughly every 15–30 seconds
- scene payoff: roughly every 60–120 seconds
- act/reversal payoff: roughly every 3–5 minutes
- final payoff: the episode's strongest emotional or intellectual resolution

Longform attention should breathe. A quiet face, environment, walk, gesture, or object can remain on screen when the scene itself carries anticipation. Do not add artificial cuts simply to imitate Shorts.

### Longform tension architecture

`HOOK → CENTRAL QUESTION → ORIENTATION → COMPLICATION → SMALL PAYOFF → DEEPER QUESTION → ESCALATION → BREATHING ROOM → REVERSAL → CONSEQUENCE → CLIMAX → PAYOFF → REFLECTION`

The intensity should rise and fall. Recovery periods are intentional and prevent fatigue; they are not filler.

### Longform visual grammar

- Establish geography before repeatedly using close shots.
- Alternate scale: wide → medium → detail → reaction → wide consequence.
- Let camera movement carry attention during quieter narration.
- Use parallax and layered environmental movement for sustained shots.
- Use ink construction selectively for meaningful discoveries, entrances, objects and emotional reveals.
- Reserve the most elaborate construction/reveal for major narrative moments.
- Use silence, restrained sound and stillness before major revelations.
- A visual beat can last ~2–5 seconds when the action, emotion or camera movement remains meaningful.

## Format-independent visual rule

AI creates the master artwork. Code creates the movie.

Never require one AI image per beat. Reuse master assets through shot grammar, crop/focus changes, subject-relative construction, parallax, foreground/background separation, camera movement and deterministic draw-on animation. Generate a new master only when the story genuinely requires a new character, location, prop, view or action that existing assets cannot cover.

## Renderer contract

The renderer should receive psychological intent such as:

- attention goal
- tension level
- curiosity level
- emotional level
- active/open loop
- withheld elements
- micro payoff
- payoff target
- pattern interrupt
- visual strategy

The renderer must remain format-agnostic at the primitive level. Format policy decides density and breathing room; visual primitives execute the resulting intent.
